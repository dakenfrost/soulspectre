Faction Culture

Souls, Contracts & The Price of Everything

An in-depth look at the values, laws and hierarchy of the Devil Syndicate, and at the forces that defend its interests.

A Transactional Culture

Mortals call devils corrupters, and they are not wrong. But a devil's corruption is rarely the work of pure malice. It is a trade: the devil wants something, the mortal wants something, and a contract makes both wishes come true.

A starving village receives bread, a dying king another decade, a hero the strength to win one last battle. In return, the devil receives what it came for, usually a soul. Devils see nothing wicked in this: nobody is forced to sign, and nothing is ever given for free, neither in Hell nor anywhere else.

The Currency of Souls

Souls are the true currency of Hell. Every soul acquired through a valid contract belongs to the devil or the house that obtained it, and its value depends on its greatness: a beggar's soul is small change, while the soul of a king, a saint or a legendary hero is a fortune that can make a career. The nobler the soul, the greater the prestige of its owner.

This is why devils despise mindless violence. A corpse cannot sign, and a war that could have been won with a contract is simply a waste of resources. Some divisions hunt for quantity, offering bread and safety to the desperate; others hunt for quality, waiting patiently for the intrepid hero who needs only one small push.

Desire Without Conscience

Most devils were born from strong emotions, and at heart they remain those emotions given shape. An envious devil covets, a proud devil dominates, a greedy devil accumulates. They do not wrestle with guilt or conscience: a devil simply does what its nature demands.

What keeps Hell from collapsing into chaos is not morality, but structure. Desire is allowed, even encouraged, as long as it flows through the proper channels: a contract, a promotion, a mission well executed. A devil may want anything it likes. It simply has to earn it.

A Society of Results

The Syndicate is, at its core, a society of results. Status is earned through performance and lost the same way: those who deliver are promoted and rewarded with territories, titles, armies and souls, while those who fail are demoted without ceremony. Even in the Courts, a noble house that stops delivering soon finds its lands transferred to someone more productive.

This breeds a culture of ambition and paperwork. Every deal is recorded, every promise is measured, and every devil knows exactly where they stand on the ladder, and how far they could fall.

The Law Above All

In Hell, the law stands above everything: above the Courts, above the executives, above personal desire. Rebels and agitators are swiftly suppressed by the forces that keep order, and those who break the system rarely get a second chance.

At the heart of that law lies the contract. Without trust there can be no exchange, and without exchange the Syndicate could not exist. A devil may hide clauses within clauses and word the truth with surgical care, but a signed contract is sacred. Breaking one is the only sin every devil, noble or executive, agrees is unforgivable.

The President and the CEOs

Lucifer presides over Hell as its Abyssal President, but he does not manage its affairs personally. For every sector and every critical mission, he appoints a Chief Executive Officer: an analytical mind chosen for efficiency above all, granted authority over every official force of the Syndicate within their mandate, noble and corporate alike. Even a Duke must obey, a fact the Courts find deeply humiliating and the CEOs find entirely reasonable.

The leaders that other factions face on the battlefield are these CEOs, each entrusted with a specific mission. Their styles vary as much as their tasks: some are polished diplomats who win more with a handshake than with an army, others prefer subtle schemes, sabotage and quiet manipulation. What they share is the obligation to deliver. A CEO who succeeds is celebrated across Hell; one who fails rarely receives a second appointment.

Diplomacy and Contempt

The Syndicate feels a genuine, if calculating, sympathy for factions that respect order and institutions while preserving their own identity. Such peoples make reliable partners, and history records alliances even with the Valkyrion Empire. Their valorous and heroic souls are also worth a fortune, should anyone ever be persuaded to sign.

Two kinds of people earn only contempt. The first are those ruled by instinct alone, like the Kharos Dominion: chaotic, unpredictable and impossible to bind with a contract they would never honor. The second are those whose rules are so rigid that they crush every desire, like the dwarves of the Iron Brotherhood: citizens reduced to empty shells, who want nothing and therefore have nothing to sell. To a devil, a soul without desire is the poorest product of all.

The Forces of the Syndicate

When contracts and persuasion are not enough, the Syndicate fields four official forces, each with its own role and its own place in the eternal tug-of-war between the Courts and the corporate wing.

House of Duty

Administration & Control

The administrators, servants and protectors of Hell's great estates and offices. Pages and Keepers guard the archives, Maids and Butlers keep officials alive, while Courtesans, Torturers and Judges handle the delicate business of persuasion. At its peak stand the Governors, who rule entire provinces.

The Black Guards

Regular Army

The Syndicate's regular army, equipped by the corporate foundries but often led by Noble Scions of the old houses: the place where blood and money meet on the battlefield. Sword-armed Soldiers and Legionaries, spear-wielding Hellguards and Dark Knights, and the Arbiters who settle disputes on the spot.

Contracted Demons

Bound Abyssal Power

Chaotic creatures of the Abyss bound by airtight contracts. The nobility despises them and the executives find them distasteful, but nobody argues with their results. Contractors summon fragments of their power, while Demons grow into Demon Lords, Overlords and, at the very top, the Abyssal Tyrant.

Eclipse Ravens

Marksmen & Assassins

The Syndicate's marksmen, spies and assassins, led by Commanders who read the battlefield like a balance sheet. From Troopers and Archers to Shades, Hitmen and the legendary Endsinger, they remove problems before those problems ever reach the negotiating table.
