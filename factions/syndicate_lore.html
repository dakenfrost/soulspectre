Faction Lore

The Infernal Archives of the Devil Syndicate

In this section, we explore the origins of the devils, the founding of Hell and the cause that still drives the Syndicate: the last great rebellion.

The First Rebellion

Before Hell, before contracts, the devils were angels. They served God in the heavens, bound by laws they had never chosen and forbidden from wanting anything for themselves. Most accepted it. Some began to ask why.

The boldest among them was Lucifer. He did not seek destruction; he sought freedom: freedom to want, to choose and to own the consequences of one's own desires. When he finally broke the chains that bound him to God, a host of angels broke theirs alongside him.

"We did not fall. We let go." — Attributed to a Duke of the First Court

The heavens called it a fall. The rebels called it the first day of their lives.

The Abyssal President

Cast out and free, the rebels needed a home of their own. Lucifer forged it from the abyss itself: Hell, a realm no god's law would ever reach. In the beginning it was little more than fire and rock, but it was theirs.

Lucifer took the title of Abyssal President: one who presides over a free people rather than rules over subjects. His authority is absolute, yet he rarely gives orders. He sets goals and expects his people to find their own way to reach them. Results are his concern; methods are theirs. Those who fail are demoted, and some simply disappear: their offices are cleared overnight, and no one ever asks where they went.

"The President does not punish failure. The President simply stops paying for it." — Internal memorandum, Office of the Abyssal President

Born of Desire

The first devils were angels, but they were not the last. Over the ages, Hell discovered that some mortals burn with a desire so strong it consumes everything else: ambition, pride, envy, love twisted into obsession. When such a soul is corrupted, it does not simply fall; it is remade, and a new devil is born. Still a person, but one shaped entirely by the desire that consumed it.

These newborn devils owe nothing to the heavens. They carry no memory of chains and no lineage to boast about, only the desire that defines them and the hunger to fulfill it.

They must never be confused with demons. Demons are not people at all: they are raw aggregates of hatred, rage and hunger, given form in the depths of the Abyss and in the Circles of Hell. Devils think, negotiate and sign; demons only want. Very rarely, a demon manages to transcend the desire that created it and becomes an Archdemon, with the power of a demon and the mind of a devil. The Syndicate despises them, but it has learned to rent their power.

Blood Against Merit

This double origin split Hell in two. The descendants of the first rebels, the angels who followed Lucifer out of the heavens, became the Infernal Courts: noble houses ruled by Dukes and Barons who consider their blood the purest proof of loyalty. To them, devils born of mortal emotions will always be upstarts.

The newborn devils found their answer in another world. Devils are not bound to a single dimension, and in one of them their agents discovered mortals who had replaced crowns with contracts and lineage with performance. The corporate wing was born from that example: a world of offices, archives and foundries where noble or common means nothing, and efficiency is the only measure of worth.

The two sides despise each other with remarkable politeness. The nobles call the executives "accountants with horns"; the executives call the nobles "legacy costs". Yet neither can do without the other: the Courts provide armies, sorcery and millennia of experience, while the corporate wing provides weapons, contracts and an endless stream of souls.

The rivalry runs deepest among the executives themselves. Pureblood heirs who were handed their positions and self-made devils who clawed their way up from nothing compete for the same missions and the same souls, and they are not above sabotaging one another to get them. Sometimes their feuds explode into open battle; far more often, a rival's plans simply, quietly, fall apart.

"A Duke conquers a province and calls it glory. We acquire it, and call it a quarterly result." — Anonymous executive, Syndicate archives

The Circles of Hell

Beyond the Courts and the offices lie the Circles of Hell. They are not prisons, and almost no one there is punished. A soul consumed by a single sin simply becomes it: in each Circle, sinners lose what remained of their humanity and turn into demons of their own vice, keeping every ability that sin ever gave them. Hell wastes nothing, and the Syndicate puts them to work.

This is what separates devils from demons. A devil is a soul that desire corrupted but did not erase; a demon is what remains when nothing but the sin is left.

  • I – Limbo – A grey, endless waiting room for souls with no dominant sin. They do not change, and nothing ever happens to them, which some consider the cruelest fate of all.
  • II – Lust – An eternal storm of longing. The lustful become Lust Demons, masters of temptation who know exactly what every heart desires.
  • III – Gluttony – Freezing, rotting swamps where the gluttonous swell into Gluttony Demons, enormous and forever hungry.
  • IV – Greed – Misers and spendthrifts become Greed Demons, who want everything and can never bear that someone else has more. The corporate wing considers it the best-run Circle in Hell.
  • V – Wrath – A boiling marsh that births the Wrath Demons, colossal beings of pure fury who grow angrier the longer they fight.
  • VI – Heresy – Fields of burning tombs for those who denied every god and every rule. What crawls out of them answers to no one, not even Hell.
  • VII – Violence – Rivers of boiling blood and forests of thorns, where murderers and tyrants become the brutes of the lower depths. Devils find them crude, but they make excellent raw material.
  • VIII – Fraud – A maze of ditches for liars, forgers and false counselors, who lose their own faces and become shapeless deceivers. Rumor has it the Syndicate's Doppelgangers come from here.
  • IX – Treachery – The only true punishment in Hell. Traitors do not transform: they are locked in the frozen lake of Cocytus for eternity, because betrayal breaks the one thing that makes every exchange possible, trust.

The Last Rebellion

Behind every contract, every acquisition and every soul lies a single purpose, written into the foundations of Hell: the last great rebellion. The first one freed the devils. The next one is meant to end the order of God itself.

To the devils, that order is built on hypocrisy. It preaches virtue while demanding obedience, promises love while forbidding desire, and calls anyone who wants something for themselves a sinner. The Syndicate does not seek to destroy goodness; it seeks to tear down a system that pretends goodness can only exist in chains.

Such a war cannot be won with fire alone. So the Syndicate gathers resources: souls, armies, knowledge and influence. Every deal brings that day a little closer, and every devil, noble or executive, knows exactly what all those contracts are for.

"Heaven calls it virtue. We call it a contract with hidden clauses." — Common saying in the offices of Hell
